Reading the Board

  • Crude is the biggest absolute shortfall (~7.5 mb/d net, ~7% of global demand) but the best-buffered. US and OECD commercial crude are still above 5-year averages. The SPR release (172 mb US + ~228 mb other IEA over 2026–27) is doing the heavy lifting.
  • LNG is the most structural: Qatar accounts for ~20% of global exports; ~17% of Ras Laffan's capacity is damaged with a 3–5 year repair window. EU storage is at 29% and injections collapsed 77% y/y. This is not a 2026 problem — it is a 2027–2028 problem.
  • Products are the sharpest price moves: jet fuel +109%, naphtha +83%, diesel +55%, gasoline +52%. Refineries can't get the right crude to the right place, and the Fujairah / Singapore / ARA data all corroborate.
  • US Henry Hub is the counter-intuitive one — down ~16% since the war started while JKM is up 88% and TTF up 67%. The driver is the US LNG export bottleneck: liquefaction terminals (Sabine, Corpus, Plaquemines, Cameron, Freeport) are running flat out at ~14 Bcf/d of capacity, and Gulf Coast pipeline routes are constrained. So the ~$15/MMBtu JKM−HH arbitrage cannot be arbitraged. Abundant US production stays stranded, meeting mild shoulder-season demand and strong renewable output; storage built 50 Bcf last week. US gas is physically decoupled from the global market until new terminals ramp (late-2026 onward).
  • China is the biggest single unknown. If they keep stockpiling +730 kb/d, the global shortfall gets worse. If they switch to destocking, it is the largest potential offset in the system.
  • The earliest tripwire is Fujairah products. At record lows and declining ~6% per week, they run out in 4–6 weeks on current trajectory. That is the regional bunker / diesel crisis signal, and the first domino if this isn't resolved.
